2025 was my first year of presenting at international conferences:

- **ENACS25**, MPI for the Physics of Complex Systems, Dresden (19–23 May): poster,
  *How Structure and Parametrization Influences Synchronization in a Power Grid Network*.
- **Dynamics Days Europe 2025**, Thessaloniki (23–27 June): talk,
  *How Parametrization Influences Synchronization in a Power Grid Network*.
- **Conference on Complex Systems 2025**, Siena (1–5 September): talk,
  *Adaptive Reinforcement and Synchronization Dynamics in Power Grid Networks*.

<!--more-->

The first two presented our study of **twelve increasingly heterogeneous grid models**. Each one
adds more real electrical parameters, and we compared two ways of measuring order: the classical
Kuramoto order parameter and a "universal" one that includes network structure. For time series, the universal
order parameter separates dynamical states better. For sweeps in the coupling strength, the classical one is more informative.

The Siena talk was about **changing the network**. Even strongly heterogeneous systems can
synchronize globally, but real improvement only comes from targeted, dynamics-aware edge
additions. Poorly chosen additions can make things worse (Braess' paradox).
The comparison of reinforcement strategies is published in
[Chaos, Solitons & Fractals](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chaos.2025.117095).
